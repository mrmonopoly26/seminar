\section{Architectural comparison of \acp{SNN} and \acp{ANN}}\label{Sec:ArchitecturalComparisonOfSNNsAndANNs}
In this section, \acp{SNN} and traditional \acp{ANN} are compared regarding its fundamentals.
This encompasses the underlying neural models and the associated signal propagation with their activation mechanisms.
\subsection{Neural models}\label{Subsec:NeuralModels}
\subsubsection{Biological neuron}
\acp{SNN} and \acp{ANN} both are inspired by biological neurons.
Fig.~\ref{fig:bioneuron} illustrates the basic anatomy of a biological neuron, which is composed of four principal components: \textit{dendrites}, \textit{soma}, \textit{axon}, and \textit{synapse}.  
Dendrites function as the input interface of the neuron by converting chemical signals from neurotransmitters into electrical signals.  
These signals are then integrated in the soma, the cell body, where the accumulation of membrane potentials determines whether an action potential is generated.  
This outcome of this process, known as neural integration, decides whether the neuron will fire.  
If the threshold is reached, the axon transmits the action potential to other neurons.  
Synapses are the structural junctions that enable communication between neurons and are typically located on dendrites, facilitating the transformation and relay of signals within the network~\cite{yamazaki_spiking_2022, pietrzak_overview_2023}.  
\acp{SNN} and \acp{ANN} model this structure in similar ways.

\subsubsection{Neurons in traditional \acp{ANN}}
A traditional artificial neuron, also known as a perceptron, is the fundamental computational unit in a \ac{ANN}.
Each artificial neuron mimics basic biological mechanisms such as input integration and threshold activation.
In a perceptron, input features \( x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n \) (and a bias term) are received and weighted by coefficients \( w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_n \), aggregated, and transformed by an activation function to produce a numeric output.
The activation function introduces non-linearity into the model, which is crucial for learning complex patterns; common activation functions include sigmoid, tanh, ReLU, and SoftMax.
A single neuron is insufficient to solve complex tasks; therefore, multiple neurons are organized into layers to form a network, where each neuron processes part of the input and contributes to the overall output.
Networks that include more than one hidden layer are referred to as deep neural networks and are particularly suited to capturing high-level data abstractions~\cite{taye_understanding_2023}.

In feedforward neural networks, a special type of \acp{ANN}, information flows in one direction from input to output, without cycles, and such networks can be understood as compositions of multiple functions \( f^{(1)}, f^{(2)}, \ldots, f^{(n)} \) forming a computation graph.
Each layer in a feedforward network consists of vector-valued computations, where each component can be viewed as a neuron-like unit that transforms its inputs into an output activation~\cite{goodfellow_deep_2016}.
Goodfellow et. al. claim that the field of \acp{ANN} is nowadays driven by engineering and mathematical disciplines rather than neuroscientific disciplines.
Although the original design of \acp{ANN} was inspired by the brain, modern \acp{ANN} primarily serve as statistical function approximators, optimized for generalization rather than for emulating biological processes~\cite{goodfellow_deep_2016}.

\acp{SNN} constitute a distinct class of artificial neural networks in which information is transmitted via sequences of discrete events known as spikes, contrasting the scalar activations found in \acp{ANN}.
% TODO explain excitatory and inhibitatory effects
A spiking neuron integrates incoming spikes over time and emits an output spike when its internal state crosses a defined threshold, a behavior which is modulated by excitatory and inhibitory synaptic inputs.
Mathematically, the spike train of a neuron is represented as \( S(t) = \sum_f \delta(t - t_f) \), where \( \delta(\cdot) \) denotes the Dirac delta function and \( t_f \) are the firing times~\cite{ponulak2_introduction_2011}.
The defining feature of SNNs is that information is encoded in the timing of spikes rather than in their shape, allowing them to more closely mimic biological neurons~\cite{ponulak2_introduction_2011, maass_networks_1997}.

Spiking neuron models such as Integrate-and-Fire, Leaky Integrate-and-Fire, Izhikevich, and Hodgkin–Huxley vary in their level of biological realism and computational complexity~\cite{pietrzak_overview_2023}.
The Integrate-and-Fire model abstracts the neuron as a system that integrates input until a threshold is reached, whereas the Leaky-Integrate-and-Fire model includes a leak term that causes the membrane potential to decay over time, making it a better fit for biological more realistic~\cite{pietrzak_overview_2023, nunes_spiking_2022}.
The Izhikevich model increases biological plausibility by capturing a broader range of spiking behaviors while remaining computationally efficient.
In contrast, the Hodgkin–Huxley model, derived from detailed experiments on squid axons, offers the highest fidelity to biological neurons but incurs significant computational cost~\cite{pietrzak_overview_2023}.
Despite being abstractions, spiking neuron models more accurately reflect the temporal dynamics and event-driven communication observed in biological systems compared to \acp{ANN}.
Typically, the neuron's membrane potential results from a weighted sum of excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials, with the neuron firing when this potential surpasses a predefined threshold~\cite{maass_networks_1997}.
Maas formalized the \ac{SNN}:
An \ac{SNN} consists of a finite set of spiking neurons \( V \), synaptic connections \( E \subseteq V \times V \), synaptic weights \( w_{u,v} \), response functions \( s_{u,v}(t) \), and threshold functions \( \Theta_v \) for each neuron \( v \in V \)~\cite{maass_networks_1997}.
Overall, the spiking neuron model provides a time-sensitive, biologically inspired framework that supports event-driven computation, making it fundamentally different from traditional artificial neurons where neurons processed continuous values.
However, the models (e.g., Integrate-and-Fire or Hodgkin-Huxley) differ regarding their biological plausibility, which leads to computationally differences.
